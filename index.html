Scaling Whole-Body Loco-Manipulation
through Compositional Data Synthesis

Abstract

Acquiring diverse whole-body dexterous manipulation data for humanoids has long been a fundamental challenge in character animation and robotics. Existing methods either rely on costly motion capture systems or synthesize motions with poor physical plausibility, hindering the acquisition of high-quality, diverse datasets at scale.

In this work, we present ManipSynth, a unified and morphology-agnostic framework for scalable whole-body loco-manipulation data synthesis. ManipSynth employs a compositional synthesis strategy: whole-body motion is generated via learning-based sparse whole-body tracking, while hand-object motion and contact transitions are synthesized through contact-aware, rule-based optimization followed by physical refinement. Using the data generated by ManipSynth, we further train ManipTracker, a general-purpose Humanoid-Object Interaction (HOI) tracking controller capable of steering physically simulated humanoids to imitate the reference HOI data.

Experiments demonstrate that ManipSynth achieves approximately 5× higher interaction coverage and 6× lower penetration than OMOMO across a range of representative objects. Meanwhile, ManipTracker attains around 90% tracking success rate on our synthetic dataset and maintains strong generalization performance.
